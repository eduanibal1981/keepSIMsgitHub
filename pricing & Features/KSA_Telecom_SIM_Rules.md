# Saudi Arabia Telecom Providers: Prepaid SIM Deactivation & Reactivation Rules

This document outlines the official regulations set by the **Communications, Space and Technology Commission (CST)** of Saudi Arabia, alongside the specific policies of major telecom providers. It serves as a guide for users to understand how to keep their prepaid SIM lines active and what happens if they are deactivated.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Universal CST Regulation (The 90-Day Rule)**
> By law, all prepaid SIM cards in Saudi Arabia require an "Active Transaction" at least once every **90 days** to remain valid. If no active transaction occurs, the telecom provider has the right to suspend and eventually permanently terminate the SIM.

## 🛡️ How to Avoid Deactivation (Validity Rules)

To keep your line active and your balance safe, you must perform at least one of the following **"Qualifying Transactions"** before your 90-day (or operator-specific) validity window expires:
- **Recharge/Top-up your balance** (This resets the 90-day timer immediately).
- **Make a paid outgoing voice call** (even a 1-second call qualifies).
- **Send a paid SMS.**
- **Consume mobile data** (from your balance or an active package).
- **Subscribe to or renew a telecom package/bundle.**

> [!TIP]
> **Auto-Recharge is the Safest Strategy:** Using the KeepSIMs Auto-Refill feature automatically performs a top-up transaction before the 90-day window closes, ensuring the SIM remains permanently active without manual intervention.

### Provider-Specific Nuances:
*   **STC (Sawa):** Requires a recharge or paid activity every 90 days. Dial `*166#` to check validity.
*   **Mobily:** Requires a recharge or paid activity every 90 days. Dial `*1411#` to check validity.
*   **Zain KSA:** Requires a recharge or paid activity every 90 days.
*   **Virgin Mobile & Lebara:** Some recharges can extend the balance validity up to 120 days, but it is highly recommended to stick to the 90-day activity rule to comply with base network CST guidelines.

## ⚠️ Deactivation & Reactivation Rules

When a SIM card passes its validity period without any qualifying transaction, it goes through a lifecycle of suspension and eventual termination.

### Phase 1: Suspension (Incoming Calls Only)
*   **What happens:** After 90 days of inactivity, the line is partially suspended. You can no longer make outgoing calls, send SMS, or use data, but you can still receive incoming calls and SMS.
*   **How to Reactivate:** The line is easily reactivated during this grace period. Simply **recharge the balance** or buy a package. The line will instantly return to fully active status, and the 90-day timer will reset.

### Phase 2: Permanent Termination (Deactivation)
*   **What happens:** If the SIM remains in the "Suspension" phase for an extended period without a recharge (typically 90 to 180 days depending on the operator, e.g., Zain gives 180 days), the CST mandates that the line be **permanently terminated**. 
*   **Consequences:**
    - The SIM card becomes permanently dead and will say "No Service".
    - Any remaining balance is permanently forfeited and cannot be refunded.
    - The phone number is completely unlinked from your Iqama/ID.
*   **How to Reactivate:** **You cannot reactivate a permanently terminated SIM card remotely.** Once terminated, the number is returned to the telecom operator's public pool to be sold to a new customer. To get the number back, you must physically visit a branch of the telecom operator and check if the number is still available in the system to be purchased as a brand-new SIM card.

> [!WARNING]
> **Visitor / Tourist SIMs (Hajj & Umrah)**
> The rules above apply to standard resident/citizen prepaid lines. Tourist, Hajj, and Umrah SIMs have strict, fixed validity periods (usually 30 to 90 days) and are **automatically and permanently deactivated** when the visa/period expires. They cannot be renewed or extended.
