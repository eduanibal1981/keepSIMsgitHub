# 6 — Competitor and Alternative Positioning

The real competitor is not another app. It is the customer's current coping behaviour. Position against behaviours first, products second. Every line below already complies with creative-brief §4 banned terms — reuse the phrasing as written; do not "improve" it back into remittance language.

## The alternatives, in order of actual market share

### 1. Doing nothing (the default)

- **Their belief:** "My number is fine. I topped it up… sometime."
- **Failure mode:** inactivity windows are invisible until the number is gone; recovery requires physical presence in the home country (creative-brief §1).
- **Position:** KeepSIMs is the difference between *assuming* and *knowing*. Sell the dashboard status ("Clear status. Less worry." — Guide §13) before selling any refill.
- **Never say:** any stated inactivity threshold as fact ("carrier rules vary — we track them for you").

### 2. Asking family back home to top up

- **Their belief:** free, trusted, already works.
- **Failure mode:** depends on someone remembering; no status visibility; no confirmation until something breaks.
- **Position:** don't attack the family — *absorb* it. "Stop assigning homework to your brother. See it, schedule it, done." Multi-line framing turns the family from the workaround into the use case (persona 5): the expat manages the family's lines instead of the family managing the expat's.
- **Trap to avoid:** never frame this as "send money home instead" — banned-terms violation. It is airtime scheduling, not money.

### 3. Carrier apps and websites

- **Their belief:** official is safest.
- **Failure mode:** one app per carrier; many block foreign cards or foreign IPs; no cross-carrier view; no automation aimed at expats.
- **Position:** "One dashboard for every line you care about" + payment that works from where you actually live. No carrier disparagement, no affiliation claims (12-compliance).

### 4. Consumer top-up services (Ding, Reloadly consumer, and similar)

- **Their belief:** proven way to push airtime abroad.
- **Failure mode:** transactional, not protective — no monitoring, no expiry visibility, no scheduling around inactivity windows, no wallet dedicated to keeping lines alive.
- **Position:** they sell a *transaction*; KeepSIMs sells a *state* — the line stays active. "Top-up sites refill a number. KeepSIMs keeps it alive." This is the sharpest competitive line in the plan; use it widely.
- **Accuracy rule:** never claim price superiority [VERIFY: price comparison vs Ding/Reloadly consumer per corridor before any price-comparison claim ships].

### 5. Remittance apps with airtime add-ons

- **Failure mode for the user:** airtime is a side feature; no protection logic.
- **Positioning rule — read carefully:** this is the highest banned-terms risk zone in the whole document. KeepSIMs must never position itself *within* their category ("like a remittance app but…" is banned framing). When comparison is unavoidable: "Those apps move value for people. KeepSIMs keeps your number active. Different job." Prefer not naming them in paid/organic assets at all; reserve this positioning for SEO articles and FAQ where the search intent already contains the comparison.

### 6. eSIM travel providers (Airalo and similar)

- **Different job entirely:** they connect you *where you are*; KeepSIMs protects the number *where you're from*. Not competitors — a complementary framing and a future partnership candidate (a traveler buying a UAE eSIM is exactly a person whose home SIM is now idle in a drawer). Position as adjacency, never confusion.

### 7. Roaming plans from the home carrier

- **Failure mode:** built for trips, priced for weeks, absurd for a 2-year contract worker; usually still requires periodic top-up to stay valid.
- **Position:** "Roaming keeps you connected for a trip. KeepSIMs keeps your number alive for the years."
