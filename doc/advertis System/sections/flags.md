# flags.md — Session 2 concerns log

Per the critical rule: nothing here reopens a Session 1 decision. These are risks and observations for the founder, logged and proceeded past. (Seeded by Session 1's model; the Opus session appends below the line.)

**F1 — Attribution is a launch gate, not a task.** No MMP integrated; iOS ATT limits paid attribution regardless. Without week-one `attribution_source` capture, the kill rule and the north star are unmeasurable, and section 9's discipline is theater. Section 13 gates all paid spend on this.

**F2 — Payments may be the real bottleneck, not marketing.** PayPal + Stripe only, and PayPal consumer coverage in Saudi/Qatar is historically limited [V9]. If live test deposits fail there, the two largest residence markets can't convert regardless of creative quality. A local method (mada / STC Pay class) may be a prerequisite for the Saudi market, which is a product decision outside this plan's authority.

**F3 — Single-line economics leave no margin for error.** At $2.60/yr contribution, even the community plan only works if content costs stay near zero marginal and referral credit stays ≤$1.50. If multi-line attach underperforms for two consecutive months, the honest move is a pricing conversation (Premium ≥ $9.99 bundle or base-fee rise per unit-economics §what-would-change), not more marketing.

**F4 — Premium is currently unadvertisable everywhere until V11 closes.** The corridor permissions (PK/BD/EG-partial) are necessary but not sufficient — there is no published Premium price to quote, and creative-brief §2 requires quoting the real yearly price for the blue-collar segment. V11 is quietly on the critical path for the plan's flagship Premium segment.

**F5 — DTone timing risk cuts both ways.** If DTone lands mid-quarter (V19), India's Premium verdict flips and significant creative/SEO work becomes extendable — but only after 04 and unit-economics are re-run. The temptation will be to pre-build India Premium assets "ready to flip on." Resist it: pre-built assets leak into distribution.

**F6 — Refund-rate ambiguity.** The 14-day refund headline (creative-brief §6) will mechanically raise refund *requests* as deposits grow. §11 flags rising refund rate as an over-promising signal — the manager needs a baseline period before treating it as an alarm, or the metric will cry wolf in month one.

**F7 — Community channel is unmeasurable without discipline.** 45% of budget flows to a channel that only attributes via promo codes and links. If admins strip links or communities forbid them, spend becomes faith-based. The no-code-no-renewal rule (section 8A/9) must actually be enforced; expect pressure to make exceptions.

**F8 — Zendit test-account status.** All current fulfillment data is from an account the owner describes as test-only. Every live top-up assumption inherits that caveat until a production transaction is receipted (V3/V4 test top-ups). Marketing cannot verify this; it can only refuse to overstate.
